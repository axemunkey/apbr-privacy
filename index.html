APBR Privacy Policy

Effective November 1, 2026 · Updated November 1, 2026 to add optional scan sharing

APBR is an app for tracking your Powerball tickets and checking them against official results. It is built to keep your information on your device. This policy explains what the app does with your data.

The short version

APBR does not collect personal information. There are no accounts, no analytics, no advertising, and no tracking. The only thing that ever leaves your device is optional: if you turn on scan sharing, parts of the tickets you scan are sent anonymously to help improve scanning (see below). It is off unless you turn it on.

Camera

APBR uses the camera only when you choose to scan a ticket. The image is read on your device using Apple's built-in text recognition. Ticket images are not uploaded unless you turn on scan sharing.

Optional scan sharing

The first time you save a scanned ticket, APBR asks whether you'd like to share scans to help make ticket scanning more accurate. You can change your answer any time under About in the app. If you turn it on, each time you save a scanned ticket APBR sends:

  • the part of the ticket photo that shows your numbers (not the barcode or serial numbers);
  • the text the app read from the ticket, with serial numbers, security codes, and retailer numbers removed;
  • the numbers the app read and the numbers you confirmed, whether Power Play and Double Play were on, the draw date, the number of draws, and the app version.

This is stored in Apple's iCloud (CloudKit) in a database that belongs to the app. It is not linked to your name, email, or Apple Account; CloudKit attaches only an anonymous identifier that is specific to this app, which is what lets you delete your own shared scans. Other users cannot see what you share. It is used only to test and improve how APBR reads tickets, and it is never sold or shared with anyone else.

Shared scans are kept until you delete them or until they are no longer needed to improve scanning. To delete everything you have shared, tap Delete scans I've shared under About in the app. Scan sharing requires being signed in to iCloud; if you aren't, nothing is sent.

Your tickets

Tickets you scan or enter, including their numbers, draw dates, results, and images, are stored only on your device; they are not synced to iCloud. Deleting a ticket, or deleting the app, removes that data from your device.

Internet access

APBR downloads public information so it can check your tickets:

  • Winning Powerball numbers from the New York State open-data service (data.ny.gov).
  • The current jackpot amount from powerball.com.

These requests ask only for public draw results. They do not include your ticket numbers or any information about you. As with any website, these services may see your device's IP address when the app connects to them, under their own privacy policies.

Notifications

If you allow notifications, APBR alerts you about winning tickets and tickets close to expiring. These alerts are created on your device. No notification server is involved.

Children

APBR is intended for adults who are legally able to purchase lottery tickets. It does not knowingly collect information from anyone, including children.

Changes

If this policy changes, the updated version will be posted on this page with a new effective date.
